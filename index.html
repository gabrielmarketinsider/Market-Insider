🌍 Morning Market Briefing

August 17, 2026

Key Market Themes

  • Global tech/AI-hardware selloff carried into the new week: Nasdaq -4.18% (25,709.43), S&P 500 -2.64% (7,383.74), Dow -1.35% (50,866.78) Friday, triggered by Broadcom's underwhelming AI-chip outlook and a stronger-than-expected jobs report that spiked Treasury yields.
  • Risk-off spilled into Asia (Nikkei -1.31%, Hang Seng -1.15%) as traders await China's July retail sales/industrial output and watch the fraying Iran ceasefire.
  • Gold (-3.1%) and crude (-2.7%) both fell despite Middle East tension, as dollar strength and higher real yields (10Y at 4.54%) dominated safe-haven flows.

FOMC / Fed Watch

Fed funds rate held at 3.50%–3.75% since the July meeting; three regional presidents (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan) dissented in favor of a hike, underscoring an internal split. Markets currently price roughly 55–65% odds of one more 25bp cut in 2026 (Sept 16–17 or October meeting most likely), with a meaningful 30–40% chance of no cut at all. Jackson Hole symposium is later this month and will be the next major catalyst for the rate path; Friday's stronger jobs data complicates the case for near-term easing by keeping yields elevated.

Geopolitical & Macro

Middle East: the 2026 Iran ceasefire (in place since April) faces its most serious test — renewed Israeli strikes on southern Lebanon/Beirut and the ceasefire's formal expiry reportedly due today, with fresh US sanctions on Iran also in play. Brent tested ~$89. Trump called off a planned bombing campaign against Iran on Aug 8 to allow more diplomacy; a 60-day Iran-Oman arrangement over Strait of Hormuz traffic was being negotiated around Aug 12. US data has softened: July retail sales posted their steepest drop in over a year, and consumer sentiment fell to 51 vs. 55 expected.

Asia News — Singapore Focus

Singapore's STI dipped modestly (~-0.35%) tracking regional weakness; DBS, UOB, and SingTel remain the key large caps to watch. China's July retail sales and industrial output land later today — spending is expected to firm modestly even as factory activity cools. South Korea's market is closed for a holiday; Hong Kong and Japan both caught in the global chip-led rout.
