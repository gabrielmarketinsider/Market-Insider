Nvidia’s $96 Billion Beat-and-Raise and a Cooling Inflation Print Send Asian Chipmakers Racing Higher as Jackson Hole Opens
Wall Street closed higher for a fourth straight session Wednesday — the S&P 500 added 0.12% to 7,686.64, the Nasdaq rose 0.08% to 26,173.36 and the Dow edged up 0.03% to 53,594.69 — even as a hotter-than-expected headline PCE print (3.7% annually) briefly weighed on morning trade. Nvidia then delivered its most consequential quarter yet after the bell: $96.2 billion in revenue (crushing the roughly $92.2 billion analyst consensus), adjusted EPS of $2.22 versus $2.09 expected, data-center revenue of $89 billion (up 117% year-on-year), and third-quarter guidance of $108 billion, about $3.8 billion above Wall Street’s $104.19 billion estimate. The stock initially slipped as much as 2% in the immediate after-hours reaction before recovering to trade as high as $217, roughly 3.5% above its $209.66 regular-session close, as CEO Jensen Huang declared “AI has reached its inflection point.” Cybersecurity firm CrowdStrike also beat and raised, with shares jumping 11.7% to $207.46. Underneath the earnings, July’s core PCE inflation matched expectations at 0.2% month-on-month and 3.3% year-on-year, trimming September rate-hike odds to 38% from 40% and cutting year-end multi-hike odds to 28% from 60% a month ago — just as Kevin Warsh’s Kansas City Fed symposium in Jackson Hole opens Thursday, building toward his first keynote as Fed chair on Friday. Thursday morning in Asia, the rally is broad-based: the Kospi is up 0.97% to 6,808.21, the Hang Seng 0.78% to 25,710, the Nikkei 225 0.61% to 66,258 and the Shanghai Composite 0.71% to 3,917.16, as chipmakers price in Nvidia’s beat and oil extends its slide on news that Iran and Oman have agreed a temporary shipping corridor through the Strait of Hormuz.

Bottom Line
Thursday’s setup is a market drawing confidence from two beat-and-raise mega-cap prints rather than de-risking into Jackson Hole. Nvidia’s recovery from an initial after-hours dip to roughly $217, together with CrowdStrike’s 11.7% pop, shows investors are still willing to pay up for AI and AI-adjacent growth even as the bar for a “good enough” quarter keeps rising. Underneath that, July’s in-line core PCE gave the Fed real breathing room — year-end odds of multiple hikes have fallen from 60% to 28% in a month — setting up Kevin Warsh’s first Jackson Hole keynote Friday as the week’s real test of whether the market’s dovish read holds. Layered on top, a modest but real de-escalation in the Strait of Hormuz is pulling oil and gold lower together, a combination worth watching for what it implies about risk appetite heading into the next 48 hours.
